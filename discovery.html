He Does Not Want Leads. He Wants An Air Con Engineer.

Call with John · 5 October 2026, 5pm · AG1 Consulting
The Brief, And It Is Not What A Marketing Call Usually Is
What Matthew told our team on 1 October
  • 🔴 Looking for staff. Indeed all summer and nobody has applied. Not a weak field. Nobody.
  • 🔴 In desperate need of an air conditioning engineer.
  • Six people including himself, plus an admin team.
  • Average job £6,000 to £10,000.
  • ⭐ £50,000 a month on materials.

⭐⭐ So this is a hiring conversation, not a lead generation one, and walking in with a pitch about enquiry volume would tell him immediately that nobody read his notes.

⚠️ And £50,000 a month on materials is the number that matters most. That is £600,000 a year of materials alone. This is not a small firm with a nice website. It is a real business that cannot grow because it cannot find one person.

⭐ Why John Is The Right Person For This Specific Call
The note says "plus an admin team". John is almost certainly the one who ran the Indeed ads

Matthew cannot make it. ⭐ For a hiring problem that is close to ideal, because the person who posted the job, paid for the listing and watched nobody apply is usually not the owner.

  • He will know exactly what was tried, how long for, and what it cost.
  • He will know how many applied, rather than the owner's impression that it was “nobody”.
  • He will know what the advert actually said, which is usually the whole problem.
  • ⭐ And he will have opinions about why it failed that Matthew has not heard.

⛔ Do not pitch him. He cannot buy. Say so at the start: “I am not going to sell you anything today, Matthew is not here. I would rather understand the hiring problem properly so that when I speak to him I am not wasting his time.” It is true and it relaxes the whole call.

🔴 The Questions That Matter, In Order
🔴 1. What exactly is the role?
  • Air con engineer covering what? Install, service and maintenance, or both?
  • Does it need F-Gas certification? ⚠️ If yes, the pool is a fraction of the size and that alone may explain the silence.
  • Domestic, commercial, or both?
  • Is it one person or more?
  • Would a solar or electrical engineer who could be trained into it work, or does it have to be someone already doing it?
🔴🔴 2. What exactly has been tried? This is the most important question on the page
  • Indeed since when, and what did it cost?
  • How many applicants, honestly? Zero, or a handful who were no good? Those are completely different problems.
  • What does the advert say? ⭐ Ask him to read the title out, or send it over.
  • Does it state the pay? ⚠️ If not, that is very likely a large part of it.
  • Anything else tried? Agencies, word of mouth, the existing team, trade groups?

⭐⭐ “Nobody applied” is almost never a shortage of people. It is usually a shortage of people seeing it, or an advert that gives them no reason to move. A skilled air con engineer is employed. He is not on Indeed on a Tuesday evening.

🔴 3. What is it costing them?
  • What work are you turning away, or not bidding for, because you are short?
  • How many jobs a month could you do with one more engineer?
  • Is the six including Matthew all engineers, or does that include office?
  • Is anyone being paid overtime to cover it?

⭐⭐ Then do the arithmetic out loud with him. At £6,000 to £10,000 a job, one extra install a month is £72,000 to £120,000 a year. ⭐ That is the whole business case for fixing the hiring problem, and it is his own number.

⚠️ 4. The things that decide whether it is fixable
  • Employed or subcontract?
  • What is the pay, roughly? ⛔ Not to judge it. If they are under the going rate, better advertising produces better candidates who then decline, and we would be spending their money to find that out.
  • Where is the work? Travel radius, and whether a van comes with it.
  • What is good about working there? ⭐ Six people and a £600,000 materials spend is a proper firm, not a two-man band. That is worth something to a candidate and it is probably not in the advert.

Question 1 decides the diagnosis. ⭐ Genuinely zero applicants means nobody is seeing it, which is a reach problem and very fixable. A handful who were no good means the advert is attracting the wrong people, which is a message problem and even more fixable. Matthew said “nobody”, but owners round down.
⚠️ One Thing We Noticed, Worth Checking
⚠️ Air conditioning does not appear on their website

We read solar-fuel.co.uk. It covers solar, battery and storage, commercial work and accreditations. We found no mention of air conditioning anywhere, and no careers or vacancies page.

⭐ Two honest possibilities, and John will know which:

  • Air con is a growing part of the business the website has not caught up with. In which case they are invisible for it to customers and to engineers.
  • Or it is a small side of what they do, and the engineer is needed for a specific contract. Which changes the whole brief.

⚠️ Ask it as a question, not a finding. “I had a look at the site and could not see air con on there. Is that a newer part of what you do?”

What We Are And Are Not
⛔ Say this plainly if hiring comes up as a thing we might help with

AG1 is a marketing company, not a recruitment agency. We will not interview anybody, assess anybody, check a reference or tell them who to take on. We cannot guarantee a hire or a number of applicants.

⭐ What we can do is the advertising half - getting the right people to see that they are hiring, and giving them a reason to want it. That is an audience and a message problem, which is our actual trade. The deciding stays with them.

⚠️ And if the honest answer is that the pay is below the going rate, we would say so rather than take their money.

What Is Genuinely Good About Them
⭐ Worth saying, and it buys you the rest of the call

MCS, NAPIT, TrustMark and RECC. All four, on their own website. ⭐ That combination is rare, it is exactly what an informed buyer checks for, and it is also a reason a good engineer would want to work there. A properly accredited firm with a £600,000 materials spend is not a risky place to take a job.

Plus thirty pages, a maintained blog, a team page, battery and storage, commercial work named, reviews and a guarantee.

⛔ There is no “your website is broken” angle here and we should not invent one. It is the best built site of anybody we are speaking to this week, and John works there.

The Political Questions
  • 1. What is your role day to day? Ask first and plainly. “Main admin” could mean office manager, operations, or the person who runs everything while Matthew sells.
  • 2. How does something like this normally get decided? ⭐ Not “who decides”, which sounds like you are trying to get past him.
  • 3. What would Matthew say the biggest problem is?
  • 4. And what would you say it is? ⭐⭐ Ask both. If Matthew says hiring and John says something else, that difference is the most useful thing you will hear.
  • 5. Has anyone pitched you marketing before, and how did it go?
How To Close It
⛔ Do not ask for the sale. Ask for the next conversation

“That is really helpful. Based on what you have told me there is something specific I would want to show Matthew about the hiring, but I would rather put something real in front of him than a general pitch. When is he about?”

⭐ Makes John an ally rather than a gatekeeper, and books the next meeting without asking him for a decision he cannot make. ⚠️ And ask whether he wants to be on it. If he has been the one running the Indeed ads, he has a stake in it going well.
